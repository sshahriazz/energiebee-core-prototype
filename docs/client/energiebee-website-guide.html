A tour of the EnergieBee website

What each page shows, why it is there, how to find your way around, and what to expect from the prototype today.

In one sentence: the website turns a home's solar panels, battery, smart meter, gas meter, car and tariff into one clear picture, half-hour by half-hour: what was made, used, bought and sold, what it cost, what it saved, and what is coming next. Every number on every page can be traced back to where it came from.

1Finding your way around

1 · Pages

The menu across the top. Pages show the home and period you picked.

2 · Home & period

Pick the home, then Today, Yesterday, 7 or 30 days.

3 · Sync

Fetch fresh data now. It also happens by itself every 10 minutes.

4 · Light / dark

Switch the colour theme. Your choice is remembered.

5 · Trust bar

How much of the period is final and how many checks agree.

Tip: hover over any chart for exact numbers, and click a bar to open the evidence behind it. Coloured banners at the top of a page flag a provider problem, demo data or unverified values.

3The pages, and why each one exists

Everyday viewplain answers in pounds for the household
My energy

This month's bill so far and a month-end estimate; the next cheapest window to use power; how much solar and battery saved; where the money went (electricity, gas, standing charges, export); a short list of things worth checking; a tariff verdict; and a comparison with Ofgem's typical home.

Why: answers “how much am I spending, am I doing well, what should I do next?” without needing to read a chart.

Home

A live diagram of power flowing between solar, battery, grid and house; current prices and a 24-hour outlook; headline tiles (cost, saved, self-sufficiency, solar, import, export, carbon); energy and price charts; a cost summary and automatic insights.

Why: the dashboard: everything important about the home on one screen.

Everyday viewcontinued
Timeline

Every part of the home (solar, battery, grid, house, EV, gas, price, cost, carbon, weather) as rows on one shared time axis: past, now and forecast, with each half-hour's data status and events such as syncs and late meter data.

Why: shows how everything relates at the same moment, e.g. the battery charging when the price drops at 23:30.

Breakdown

An energy-flow diagram (who produced, who used), a table of every flow in kWh and money, a step chart from “grid-only” cost down to the actual bill, the price per kWh by source, an average day, and carbon impact.

Why: explains the bill line by line, so a saving is never just a single unexplained figure.

Each part of the systemone page per piece of equipment or cost
Solar

What the panels made against the forecast and the weather, where each kWh went (home, battery, export), what it was worth, and what is expected next. Why: shows whether the panels perform as they should.

Battery

Charge level, energy in and out, the cost of the stored energy and the saving it made, and one half-hour worked through in full. Why: proves the battery pays its way.

Grid

Energy bought and sold, prices paid, tariffs in force, upcoming prices, and a daily check of smart meter against inverter. Why: the money side of the grid connection.

Tariffs

Rates and taxes in force, each labelled with its source; then the home's real use re-priced on Agile Octopus and Agile + Agile Outgoing, with who wins, by how much and when. Why: answers “am I on the right tariff?” with the home's own data.

EV

Car charging sessions, their cost, the share powered by sunshine, and planned smart-charge slots. Why: the car is often the biggest single load.

Proof & behind the scenesfor anyone who wants to check the working
Data & trust

How much of the period is final, how independent measurements agree, every kWh accounted for each day, and every change made to a finished day. Why: trust is shown, not claimed.

Data Explorer

Tabs for Overview, Raw data, Facts, Timeline, Slot inspector and Correlations: follow any half-hour from the provider's original file to the final figure. Why: full audit trail.

Connectors

All 14 data connectors with a status each (connected, error, available, not built), problems first. Why: shows at a glance which data feeds are healthy.

Compatibility

A live check that the database can hold everything the Predbat and PredHeat planners read and write: today 456 of 456 verified items. Why: readiness for battery and heating planning.

4What to expect today

Working now
  • Live data from the inverter and Octopus, refreshed every 10 minutes
  • Electricity, gas, standing charges and export in pounds
  • Solar and usage forecasts; tariff comparison
  • Every number traceable to its source
Good to know
  • Smart-meter data arrives about a day late; recent half-hours show as provisional until it does
  • If a provider fails, a red banner names it; other pages keep working
  • This is a prototype: one live home in Great Britain, no user logins
Not built yet
  • The planner that would schedule the battery (the database is ready for it)
  • Sending commands to devices
  • EV charging figures for this car: Octopus smart control is unavailable for it
