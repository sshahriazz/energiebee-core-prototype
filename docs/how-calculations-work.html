EnergieBee core, visualized

How data arrives, where it is stored, and how one half-hour of energy and cost is calculated. All numbers are real, taken from 10 Acresbrook in your data/core.db.

The data pipeline

Data moves left to right. Pages never call a provider: they only read what the pipeline has built.

1
Save the raw response firstEvery response is kept unchanged with a hash. A repeated response is skipped.
2
Never overwrite a factA changed value is added as a new version. The old one is stamped, not deleted.
3
Serving tables are disposableThe same facts and method always give the same rows, so they can be rebuilt any time.

The database in four sentences

Read these first. The diagram below shows the same four ideas.

1

A home (site) has many devices (meters, inverter) and connections (provider logins).

2

A series is one metric, from one source, about one home or device. Every measurement belongs to a series.

3

Every fact and every price row points to the raw_payload it came from.

4

site_hh and site_day are built per home from facts and prices, and can be rebuilt any time.

One half-hour, step by step

Sun 18 Oct 2026, 12:30–13:00. Code: src/core/build.js, method v3-stock-follows-charge.

  1. 0Collect
  2. 1Align
  3. 2Reconcile
  4. 3Allocate
  5. 4Price
  6. 5Check

0Collect

Five provider responses fed this half-hour:

  • Sunsynk: 6 samples × 7 values = 42 readings
  • Octopus meter: import 0.535, export 0.010 kWh
  • Octopus gas meter: 0.314 m³
  • NESO carbon: 12 g/kWh
  • Octopus tariff: unit price and standing charge

1Align: power becomes energy

1,345 W × 0.5 h = 0.673 kWh fitted to the day's inverter counter → 0.703 kWh

2Reconcile: one value per flow

The smart meter beats the inverter's clamp. Home use is the inverter's measured load.

balance = solar + import + battery out − export − battery in = 0.703 + 0.535 + 1.730 − 0.010 − 0 = 2.958 kWh home = measured load = 2.731 kWh loss = 2.958 − 2.731 = 0.227 kWh

3Allocate: who fed whom

Solar first, then battery, then grid. What the home doesn't use is loss.

4Price: what it cost, and what it saved

Import 32.14p/kWh, VAT 0%. Gas 7.74p/kWh plus 5% VAT. The battery's energy was already paid for when it charged.

5Check and state

Checks: energy balance, meter against clamp, counter fit, price found. Then each half-hour gets one state:

final ✓Meter data is in and the half-hour is before today. This example.

missingNo grid reading from any source.

    Things to watch

    Nothing here is dummy data, but these are worth knowing.

    Panel angles may be estimatesTilt 20° and direction 60° are in .env as installer values, but may have been estimated from the home's output. Quartz uses them.

    !

    One Octopus response quarantinedAn EV-charging response on 23 Oct failed its checks and was not loaded. The other four loaded normally.

    !

    Tariffscanner's VAT basis is unknownIts API does not say whether prices include VAT. They are stored as if they don't.

    !

    A two-day gap, filledThe server was off from 09:17 on 26 Oct to 05:11 on 28 Oct. On restart each provider reached back and filled the gap.

    Three problems

    Worst first. None of them changes the import bill, which always comes from the meter. To confirm each one yourself, see “How to check this yourself” below.

    High

    An hour missing on the clock-change day is spread over the whole day

    On 25 Oct the clocks went back, so 01:00–01:59 happened twice. Sunsynk's 5-minute data has it only once: one hour of samples is missing. Sunsynk's daily counter (12.2 kWh imported) and the meter (12.08) agree; only the samples are short (8.70).

    The engine scales the day's samples up to the counter, so the missing hour's battery charging (× 1.45) is spread over other half-hours. At 00:30–01:00 the battery is shown charging 2.19 kWh from 1.71 imported, and home use shows 0. In the missing hour, the 3.2 kWh imported to charge the battery is counted as home use.

    Fix: when a day's samples have a gap, don't scale the whole day; leave the gap's split as unknown (or give it the counter's remainder), and flag the half-hours. build.js:389–391
    Medium

    Some export is never paid

    Export is credited only when solar or the battery can account for it. In 44 half-hours, 1.07 kWh of measured export got no credit, about 13p this month. Worst case: 3 Oct, 11:00–11:30, 0.375 kWh exported but only 0.183 kWh credited.

    Fix: credit the measured export × the export rate, as the supplier does. build.js:535
    Low

    Battery charging with no source

    In 68 half-hours the battery charged more than solar and the grid supplied: 5.5 kWh in total. That energy enters the battery at no cost, so energy used from the battery later looks a little cheaper than it was. They are spread over most days, a few each, where the scaled-up charge is more than solar and the meter's import; 25 Oct has 1.6 kWh of it.

    Fix: count the excess as energy of unknown cost and flag the half-hour, instead of letting it in free. build.js:476, 511

    To confirm: electricity VAT 0% from 1 Oct 2026

    The engine charges 0% VAT on electricity from 1 Oct and 5% on gas. The 0% isn't from an official source; it was read from Octopus's own prices.

    434Octopus electricity prices before 1 Oct, 5% apart with and without VAT
    5,044Octopus prices since 1 Oct, all the same with and without VAT
    If the rate is really 5%, every electricity cost since 1 Oct is 5% too low. Confirm it with HMRC, then update VAT_RATES in src/db/registry.js.

    How to check this yourself

    Don't take this tab's word for it. Three ways to check, from quickest to most independent.

    1 · Same codeThe app's slot inspectorRecomputes one half-hour and shows every step. It proves the result is repeatable, not that the rules are right.
    2 · Same dataYour own sumsRun the queries below on data/core.db, or rework a half-hour with a calculator.
    3 · Outside sourcesBills and the providers' own appsDifferent people, different code. This is the real test.

    1 · Slot inspector

    With the server running, open localhost:4100/explorer.html#slot=1792929600&site=1 (25 Oct, 12:00). It shows the samples, scale factors, meter readings, flows, prices and battery stock, and a “Matches the stored timeline” badge.

    2 · Your own sums

    From the project folder. Each line is one query; the expected answer is underneath.

    # the seven balances (0 = never off) sqlite3 -readonly data/core.db "SELECT COUNT(*), SUM(ABS(solar_to_home+battery_to_home+grid_to_home-home)>1e-6), SUM(ABS(grid_import*import_rate_ex*(1+import_vat)+standing_cost+IFNULL(gas_cost,0)-export_credit-total_cost)>1e-6), SUM(ABS(solar_to_grid+battery_to_grid-grid_export)>1e-4), SUM(ABS(solar_to_battery+grid_to_battery-battery_charge)>1e-4) FROM site_hh WHERE site_id=1" → half-hours | 0 home | 0 cost | 44 export | 68 charge
    # problem 1: the missing hour (coverage 0 = no Sunsynk samples) sqlite3 -readonly data/core.db "SELECT datetime(slot_start,'unixepoch'), json_extract(lineage,'$.coverage'), grid_import, battery_charge, home FROM site_hh WHERE site_id=1 AND slot_start BETWEEN 1792879200 AND 1792900800" → 01:00 and 01:30 UTC: coverage 0, home = all the import
    # daily totals to compare with the apps below sqlite3 -readonly -column data/core.db "SELECT local_date, solar, home, grid_import, grid_export, gas, total_cost/100 FROM site_day WHERE site_id=1"
