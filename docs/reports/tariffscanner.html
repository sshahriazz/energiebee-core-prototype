EnergieBee core · how it works

How EnergieBee knows what your electricity costs

To show a home's real cost for every half-hour, we need the price it pays. Octopus customers give us that through their account. For everyone else, we use Tariffscanner: a catalogue of every UK supplier's published tariffs. This page explains what it is, why we use it and what happens step by step.

01 · The problem

No price, no cost

Every saving and every cost figure in EnergieBee is energy × price, worked out for each half-hour. We always know the energy, from the inverter or the smart meter. The price depends on who the home buys electricity from, and there are two ways to find it.

Home on Octopus Energy

We read it from the account

  1. 1The user signs in with their Octopus login.
  2. 2Octopus tells us the exact tariff the meter is on, and since when.
  3. 3We fetch that tariff's prices. Tariffscanner isn't needed.
Any other supplier: British Gas, E.ON, EDF, OVO…

The user picks it from Tariffscanner

  1. 1We can't log in to these suppliers, so we don't know the tariff.
  2. 2Tariffscanner lists every supplier's published tariffs and prices, for every region.
  3. 3The user chooses supplier → tariff → how they pay from that list, and we price the home with it.
💡

In one sentence: Tariffscanner is our price list for the whole UK market, so a home that isn't on Octopus can still see real costs. Without it, sign-up would say “Without it, your costs cannot be worked out.”

02 · The big picture

Three moments, and only one talks to Tariffscanner

We never call Tariffscanner while a user is waiting. Once a day we copy its catalogue into our own database. Everything the user sees is read from that copy.

  • Sign in to Tariffscanner with EnergieBee's own account
  • Ask what tariffs changed since the last sweep
  • Save the answer, then store each tariff and its prices

04 · Behind the scenes

What happens in the daily sweep

The sweep runs inside the normal sync, once a day at most, for whichever home is synced first, because the catalogue is shared by all homes.

  1. 🔑

    Sign in Tariffscanner

    EnergieBee signs in with its own account (TARIFFSCAN_USERNAME / PASSWORD in .env, not the user's). The pass it gets back lasts 24 hours and is reused, so we rarely sign in twice.

  2. 📨

    Ask for what changed Tariffscanner

    “Give me active tariffs updated since the day before my last complete sweep.” Answers come in pages of up to 1,000 tariffs. Yesterday's sweep needed 2 pages.

  3. 🗄️

    Keep the original answer EnergieBee

    Each page is saved exactly as received (raw_payload + a file under data/raw/), so every stored price can be traced back to the answer it came from.

  4. 🔁

    Translate into our own format EnergieBee

    Each record becomes a tariff plus its prices: a unit price (p/kWh) and a standing charge (p/day) for each payment method. Regions are converted to the standard UK numbering. Supplier names are linked so the picker can group by supplier.

  5. 🛡️

    Never overwrite history EnergieBee

    A price that hasn't changed is left alone. A changed price closes the old one and adds the new one, so a past day is always priced at what it cost then. Nothing is deleted.

  6. ✅

    Done until tomorrow EnergieBee

    The result is recorded (“complete incremental sweep…”). The next sweep may start 24 hours after this one started, even if this one failed.

The very first sweep

On a new database, the sweep reads every active tariff, one region at a time across all 14 UK regions. It saves its place after each page, so if a run hits its request limit, the next run carries on where it stopped.

Every sweep after that

Only changes are fetched: new tariffs and new prices. That keeps each daily sweep to a few requests, well inside Tariffscanner's limits.

05 · Playing by the rules

Being a polite guest of the API

Tariffscanner limits how fast one account may ask. The limit belongs to the account, so anything else using the same login (a colleague, a script, a second copy of the server) uses up the same allowance.

20 per minute

The most requests Tariffscanner allows per account.

3 s apart

We always leave at least 3 seconds between two requests. The wait is saved in the database, so even a restarted server can't send a burst.

60 per run

A hard budget per sweep, the sign-in included. When it runs out, the sweep stops cleanly and the next one continues.

429 → stop

If Tariffscanner says “too many requests”, we stop at once, wait at least 60 seconds (longer if Tariffscanner asks) and don't retry until the next sweep.

🚦

What the red “tariffscanner … HTTP 429 (rate limited)” banner means

Tariffscanner refused one request because the account's per-minute allowance was used up. EnergieBee stopped as designed, and the home's other data kept syncing. The catalogue already held stays usable, so users can still pick tariffs. The banner clears after the next successful daily sweep. If it keeps coming back, check that nothing else is using the same Tariffscanner login at the same time.

07 · Good to know

What Tariffscanner doesn't cover

⏱️ Time-of-use tariffs are skipped

Half-hourly, smart time-of-use and similar tariffs arrive without their time pattern, so we can't price them and they're counted and skipped. Octopus Agile and similar are priced through the Octopus account instead.

🧾 VAT basis isn't stated

Prices are stored exactly as Tariffscanner publishes them, and the app treats them as before VAT. Tariffscanner's records don't say which, so this is our assumption.

🗑️ Withdrawn tariffs aren't spotted

We don't receive a list of deleted tariffs, so a tariff that disappears upstream keeps its last prices here. That's fine for pricing past days, which is what matters.

💳 Payment method matters

The same tariff often costs more when paid by cash or cheque than by Direct Debit, so we ask how the user pays and price with that method.

📈 Estimated annual cost isn't stored

Tariffscanner's yearly estimate assumes a typical home, not a price, so we don't keep it. Real costs come from real usage.

🔄 Octopus takes over

If a user later connects an Octopus account, its exact tariff replaces the picked one from then on. Earlier days keep the price the user chose.

08 · Questions

Frequently asked

Does a user need a Tariffscanner account?

No. EnergieBee uses its own account, set in .env. Users never see or enter a Tariffscanner login.

Does picking a tariff send anything to Tariffscanner?

No. Listing suppliers, listing tariffs and saving a choice all read and write our own database. Only the daily sweep talks to Tariffscanner.

The picker says “no Tariffscanner tariffs are held for this region yet”. Why?

Either the first sweep hasn't reached that region yet (it fills all 14 over one or more runs), or the home's postcode couldn't be matched to a region. Wait for the next daily sweep, or check the home's postcode.

What if a supplier changes its prices?

The next daily sweep picks up the new price. Days before the change keep the old price; days after use the new one. Nothing is overwritten.

Can a user change their tariff later?

Yes, from “Your providers”. The new tariff applies from the date they give. Earlier days keep the previous tariff, and the affected days are rebuilt straight away.

What happens if Tariffscanner is down or rate-limits us?

That day's sweep stops and records the error (the red banner). Everything already stored keeps working: pickers, pricing and rebuilds. The next sweep is tried 24 hours later.

Is Tariffscanner used outside the UK?

No. It's the UK (GB) catalogue only. France and the Netherlands are planned to use a different source (tounify) through the same picker screen.

🔎

In short: the Tariffscanner price is fixed once the tariff is chosen, so today's half-hours are priced straight away. Today's energy figures are the raw 5-minute readings; once the day is over they are scaled to the inverter's own daily totals, which can move each half-hour's cost slightly. That's why today is “provisional” and a finished day is “final”.
